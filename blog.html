How to Choose the Right Fade for Your Face Shape

Published 3 July 2026 · By The Blade & Fade Team

Walk into any salon and ask for "a fade," and you'll get a dozen different results depending on who's holding the clippers. A fade isn't one haircut — it's a family of styles, and the right one depends heavily on your face shape, hair texture, and how much daily upkeep you're willing to put in.

If you have a round face, a high or skin fade adds visual height and sharpens the jawline, especially when paired with more length on top. Square-jawed clients usually look best with a mid fade, which softens strong angles instead of exaggerating them. For oval faces — the easiest shape to style — almost any fade works, so the decision usually comes down to how much maintenance you want between visits: a skin fade needs a touch-up every two to three weeks, while a low taper fade can stretch to four or five.

Hair texture matters just as much as face shape. Thick, coarse hair holds a sharper fade line and looks cleaner with a skin fade, while fine or thin hair often looks better with a taper, since the softer blend hides gaps more forgivingly. Curly hair needs a slightly different clipper technique altogether — our barbers adjust guard length and blending angle specifically for curl pattern, not just length.

The simplest way to get it right the first time is to bring a reference photo, but also be upfront about your hair type and how often you can realistically come in for a touch-up. Our full service list outlines every fade style we offer, and you can always ask your stylist for a recommendation before the clippers come out. For a deeper technical breakdown of fade terminology, the Wikipedia entry on barbering is a solid starting reference.

Hair Care Guide

How to Keep Coloured Hair Looking Fresh Between Salon Visits

Published 25 July 2026 · By The Blade & Fade Team

Colour-treated hair fades faster than most clients expect, and it's rarely the colour itself that's the problem — it's what happens to it in the weeks after the appointment. Hot water, sulphate shampoos and direct sun exposure all strip colour molecules out of the hair shaft faster than normal wear would.

The single biggest change that extends colour life is switching to a sulphate-free, colour-safe shampoo and washing in lukewarm rather than hot water. Heat opens the hair cuticle and lets colour rinse out with every wash, while sulphates strip both natural and artificial pigment. A weekly hair spa or deep-conditioning mask also helps seal the cuticle back down between visits.

Heat styling matters just as much as washing habits. Blow-dryers, straighteners and curling irons all fade colour and dry out the hair further if used without a heat-protectant spray first. If you colour regularly, ask your stylist about a bond-repair treatment alongside your service — it keeps the hair structurally strong enough to take colour without becoming brittle.

Most clients can stretch a global colour to six to eight weeks with the right home care, though root touch-ups may be needed sooner depending on regrowth speed. If your colour is looking dull or brassy before that, our hair spa and conditioning service is a good in-between refresh, or check our contact page to book your next colour appointment.

Why Regular Beard Trims Matter More Than You Think

Published 18 July 2026 · By The Blade & Fade Team

Most men treat beard trims as an afterthought — something to squeeze in only when the beard starts looking obviously messy. In reality, a beard left untrimmed for too long doesn't just get longer, it gets uneven, patchy at the edges, and much harder to shape once it's overgrown.

A beard grows at different rates across the jaw, cheeks, and neckline, which means the outline you set during your last trim slowly disappears as some areas outpace others. Left alone for six or eight weeks, that uneven growth turns a clean beard line into a blurry one, and fixing it usually means trimming shorter than you'd like just to even everything back out.

We recommend a trim every two to three weeks for most beard lengths, with the neckline and cheek lines cleaned up even between full trims if you're growing length on the chin and moustache. Straight razor edging, rather than clippers alone, keeps the line sharp against the skin and lasts noticeably longer before it starts to look soft.

Beard health also depends on what happens between visits — regular washing, a light beard oil to prevent dry, brittle hair, and combing against the grain occasionally to catch stray hairs your barber might miss. If it's been more than a month since your last shape-up, check our contact page to book a slot before your next event, or add a beard trim onto your next haircut.
